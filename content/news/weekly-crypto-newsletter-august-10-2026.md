### Week of August 10, 2026

---

Welcome back to another edition of Fergulati Finds, your weekly source for the most important developments across Bitcoin, blockchain, and the broader digital asset ecosystem. This week brought a mix of market consolidation, institutional moves, regulatory setbacks, and a few genuinely exciting network milestones. Let us get into it.

---

## Bitcoin Holds Steady as Macro Data Shifts the Fed Narrative

Bitcoin closed the week trading at approximately \$64,736, a modest 2.15% gain over seven days. The broader market has now recovered from its earlier cycle low near \$2.05 trillion in total market capitalization, though it still sits well below the peak of \$4.27 trillion recorded in October 2025.

The week's most market-moving data came not from crypto but from Washington. The July U.S. jobs report revealed that the economy shed 23,000 positions while unemployment ticked up to 4.1%. That number badly missed expectations, and it effectively weakens the argument for any additional Federal Reserve rate increases. As has been well established, softer rate environments tend to support risk assets; Bitcoin included. Traders are watching the next Fed meeting closely, and the mood is cautiously optimistic.

Ethereum also held its ground, trading at \$1,912.62 with a 2.03% weekly gain, while Solana rose 3.86% to \$75.96. The broad stability across major assets suggests that despite the pullback from late 2025 highs, conviction in the ecosystem has not meaningfully eroded.

## Strategy Sells Bitcoin for the First Time in Years

In what was perhaps the most surprising headline of the week, Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) sold approximately 1,638 BTC for roughly \$104.7 million, cutting its holdings to 842,138 coins. The average sale price was \$63,957 per coin, which sits below the firm's cost basis of \$75,419 per coin.

Importantly, executive chairman Michael Saylor was quick to clarify that this was not a retreat from the company's Bitcoin thesis. Around \$81 million from the proceeds went toward repurchasing preferred shares and covering dividends on its STRC preferred stock. This was a liquidity management decision; one rooted in corporate obligations rather than a loss of conviction. Saylor also publicly denied reports that the company had approved a fresh plan to sell up to \$5 billion in Bitcoin, noting that figure referred to a capital management framework announced back in June.

Strategy remains one of the largest corporate holders of Bitcoin on the planet. This sale is a small chapter in a very long book.

## Fannie Mae Enters the Crypto Mortgage Market

One of the more consequential mainstream moments in crypto this cycle arrived when Fannie Mae, the government-backed mortgage giant, began accepting cryptocurrency-backed mortgages through a partnership with Coinbase and Better Home and Finance.

The program allows borrowers who hold Bitcoin or USDC on Coinbase to pledge those assets as collateral for a down payment without needing to sell their holdings and trigger a taxable event. The structure involves two loans, and borrowers must maintain a Coinbase account throughout the process. Rates on these mortgages will run 0.5 to 1.5 percentage points above a standard 30-year loan, depending on the borrower's profile. A nationwide rollout is underway this summer.

The significance of Fannie Mae's involvement should not be understated. As a federally backed institution, its participation signals that crypto-backed lending is no longer a fringe idea. It is becoming infrastructure.

## The CLARITY Act Is Officially Dead for 2026

After months of negotiations, delays, and revised drafts, the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act is effectively off the table until the next Congress. The U.S. Senate departed for its August recess without advancing the 616-page framework, stalled by an unresolved ethics provision that blocked the 60 votes needed for cloture.

Seven Senate Democrats had already signaled their opposition, citing insufficient protections on stablecoin rules and DeFi-related Anti-Money Laundering provisions. Missing the pre-recess window, as market observers noted, effectively resets any chance of comprehensive digital asset market structure legislation before the end of 2026.

For crypto businesses operating in the United States, this prolongs a familiar and frustrating uncertainty around exchange classification, custody regulation, and oversight of decentralized finance platforms. The regulatory environment remains fragmented; and that fragmentation has real costs for both entrepreneurs and institutional participants who need legal clarity before committing capital to new structures.

## Cardano Hits a Decentralization Milestone

Cardano made meaningful news this week by reaching a record Nakamoto coefficient of 16, according to data from the analytics platform Chainspect. The Nakamoto coefficient measures how many independent parties would need to collude to compromise a blockchain network; the higher the number, the more distributed and censorship-resistant the system. ADA rose nearly 20% on the news, briefly trading above \$0.199.

Beyond the coefficient record, Input Output, Cardano's core development firm, announced it will begin transferring control of key protocol components including the Haskell node, the Plutus smart contract platform, and the Daedalus wallet to external specialist teams. This is part of a multi-year initiative to achieve full development decentralization, meaning no single team controls the direction of the chain.

For those who have watched Cardano's methodical and research-first approach over the years, this week felt like genuine progress on a long-standing commitment.

## Hyperliquid Commands 70% of On-Chain Derivatives Volume

If you have not been watching Hyperliquid, this is your reminder to pay attention. The decentralized perpetual exchange, built on its own Layer 1 blockchain, now accounts for roughly 70% of all on-chain perpetual futures volume across every major blockchain, processing over \$10.5 billion in daily trading. Its native HYPE token has delivered returns exceeding 1,800% since its November 2024 launch.

What makes Hyperliquid compelling is not just the volume. It is the model. Orders are processed in under 100 milliseconds; trading is self-custodied; and zero maker fees attract sophisticated traders who previously saw no reason to leave centralized exchanges. The platform also now offers perpetual futures on real-world assets including oil, gold, and S&P 500 futures through its HIP-3 market, bridging traditional finance and decentralized infrastructure.

Three spot HYPE ETFs launched on U.S. exchanges earlier this summer, pulling in over \$100 million combined within weeks. The institutional signal is clear.

## USDC Dominates Stablecoin Volume with 70% Market Share

Circle's USDC had a landmark first half of 2026. Adjusted transaction volume on the USDC network reached roughly \$12 trillion between January and June, representing nearly 70% of all stablecoin transfers. In June alone, adjusted stablecoin transaction volume hit a record \$1.79 trillion, with USDC accounting for \$1.21 trillion of that figure.

The supply of USDC reached \$75.3 billion, a 72% increase compared to last year. Circle's quarterly on-chain volume grew 247% compared to the same period in 2025. Major institutions including Standard Chartered and Bank of New York Mellon are expanding their USDC-based services; a clear indicator that stablecoin infrastructure is no longer speculative territory but a genuine settlement layer for global finance.

## DEEP RESEARCH | Ethereum's Layer 2 Ecosystem: The Architecture of Crypto's Financial Future

*Each edition, Fergulati Finds takes a closer look at one topic that deserves more than a headline. This week: the Ethereum Layer 2 ecosystem and what its rapid consolidation tells us about where crypto infrastructure is heading.*

---

### The Settlement Layer Is Winning

For most of crypto's history, a single question dominated the Ethereum conversation: how do you scale? The answers, over time, coalesced into a specific architectural bet. Rather than pushing every transaction through the Ethereum mainnet, activity would move to Layer 2 networks, compact chains that inherit Ethereum's security while offering dramatically faster and cheaper execution. As of mid-2026, that bet is paying off in ways that are reshaping the entire industry.

As of May 2026, 73 active rollups collectively secure more than \$48 billion in total value locked. That number was under \$4 billion as recently as 2023. The growth is not speculative; it is capital in motion, flowing from developers and institutions into the parts of the ecosystem that offer practical utility at viable cost.

### Arbitrum and Base Are Running Away with the Market

The Layer 2 space is large, but it is not evenly distributed. Arbitrum One holds approximately \$16.9 billion in TVL, commanding 40 to 44% of the entire Layer 2 market. Base, Coinbase's L2, follows at around \$12.8 billion. Together, those two chains account for approximately 77% of all L2 DeFi liquidity. Analysts project 40 to 60% additional growth in combined TVL across these platforms in 2026 as new application-specific chains and cross-chain DeFi composability draw more users in.

This consolidation matters because it signals that the Layer 2 race is not a long tail of equally viable competitors. It is a market with clear leaders, and capital is clustering accordingly. Developers choosing where to deploy are making increasingly consequential decisions that favor ecosystems with liquidity depth over those with technical novelty alone.

### The Architecture Divide: Optimistic vs. ZK Rollups

In 2026, two major architectural schools define the Layer 2 landscape. Optimistic rollups, including Arbitrum, Optimism, and Base, assume transactions are valid by default and use a challenge period for dispute resolution. They offer easier developer migration from Ethereum mainnet and deep existing liquidity. Zero knowledge rollups, including zkSync Era and Starknet, use cryptographic proofs to verify every transaction, offering faster finality and higher theoretical throughput.

The short answer on which approach wins is that neither has decisively. Optimistic rollups currently dominate on liquidity and developer adoption; ZK rollups are gaining ground on performance and security guarantees. Most informed observers believe the ecosystem will ultimately support both, with each serving different use cases rather than one displacing the other entirely.

### What This Means for Ethereum Itself

One of the more counterintuitive findings of 2026 is that Ethereum's own metrics tell a mixed story even as the broader Ethereum ecosystem thrives. DEX volume on the mainnet fell 36.13% over the trailing 30 days; median mainnet transaction fees have dropped more than 99% from above \$2 in early 2024 to under \$0.02 in March 2026. By some traditional measures, Ethereum looks quieter than it once did.

However, that quietness is the point. Ethereum is no longer competing to be the execution layer for every transaction. It is functioning as a settlement layer, a trust anchor, a liquidity hub, a staking network, and the foundation for a growing web of application-specific chains. Total value locked in Ethereum's DeFi ecosystem stood at \$41.84 billion as of August 8, up 7.82% over the trailing 30 days. U.S. spot Ethereum ETFs attracted \$244.9 million in net inflows for the week ending August 7; marking five consecutive weeks of positive flows.

### Why This Matters for Crypto Consulting Clients

The Layer 2 ecosystem is where the next generation of crypto applications will be built. If you are a business evaluating blockchain integration, a developer choosing a deployment environment, or an investor trying to understand where value will accrue, the Layer 2 landscape demands your attention. Arbitrum and Base are not just technical products; they are emerging platforms with network effects, capital depth, and institutional backing. Choosing the right environment for your use case in 2026 is not unlike choosing which cloud provider to build on in 2012. The decisions made now will define competitive positioning for years to come.

The Ethereum ecosystem is maturing in the way that durable infrastructure matures: through consolidation, specialization, and the gradual replacement of novelty with genuine utility. That is not a story about hype. That is a story about a technology finding its place.
