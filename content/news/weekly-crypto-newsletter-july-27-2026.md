## Robinhood Chain Goes Live: Tokenized Stocks Now Trade 24/7 Across 120 Countries

Robinhood launched the public mainnet of Robinhood Chain on July 1 at a London event. The network is an Arbitrum based Ethereum Layer 2 built to run 100 millisecond block times and settle to Ethereum for security. At launch the chain shipped integrations with Uniswap and Chainlink and supports tokenized equities including NVDA and AAPL available via Robinhood Wallet.

Stock Tokens on Robinhood Chain can be traded around the clock used as collateral in DeFi lending pools or deployed into yield strategies. The network is available across more than 120 countries positioning it as one of the first mass market bridges between traditional equity markets and decentralized finance infrastructure. Robinhood describes the chain as AI native which means it also supports autonomous trading by AI agents as a first class capability.

The launch matters beyond Robinhood's existing user base. It signals that major fintech platforms are no longer treating blockchain as an experiment but as core infrastructure for the next generation of financial products.

## Policy & Regulation

Regulation

## The GENIUS Act Turns One: Stablecoin Rulemaking Is Still in Progress

One year ago on July 18, 2025, the GENIUS Act became the first federal law to establish a comprehensive regulatory framework for payment stablecoins. The law prohibits any entity that is not a permitted payment stablecoin issuer from offering stablecoins in the United States and subjects permitted issuers to Bank Secrecy Act anti-money laundering requirements.

The law does not treat stablecoins as securities which was a major win for the industry. Foreign issuers can still serve U.S. customers through digital asset service providers provided Treasury certifies that their home jurisdiction maintains comparable regulatory standards.

One year on multiple federal agencies are still working through parallel rulemaking processes all targeting a statutory deadline that has technically already passed. No agency has finalized its rules. That gap between the law on paper and the rules in practice creates continued ambiguity for issuers planning product launches or U.S. market entry. Expect the next 90 days to bring significant clarification as the rulemaking clock keeps ticking.

Legislation

## Clarity Act Races Toward August Deadline as Senate Ethics Fight Threatens Bipartisan Deal

The Clarity Act is the most comprehensive U.S. crypto market structure bill ever to reach this stage of legislative consideration. The House passed its version with a 294 to 134 vote in July 2025 drawing support from every Republican and 78 Democrats. The Senate Banking Committee approved its version in May 2026 by a 15 to 9 vote.

The bill now sits in a final negotiation window before Congress leaves for most of August. A newly released version includes ethics provisions for federal officials that would bar them from issuing or profiting off digital assets. Key Democratic holdouts including Senators Alsobrooks and Gallego are conditioning their votes on stronger ethics language.

The August 10 date is being watched as the practical last chance for passage this session. Industry advocates are lobbying hard across both sides of the aisle. A successful vote would establish the first clear federal framework for how digital assets are classified and traded in the United States. A failure would push the timeline well into 2027 and create continued regulatory uncertainty across the entire industry.

SEC

## SEC Chair Paul Atkins Targets Three Major Crypto Rulemaking Proposals for July

SEC Chair Paul Atkins has set July 2026 as a target window for three significant crypto rulemaking proposals. The proposals cover token offerings, broker-dealer custody of digital assets, and market structure for crypto trading venues. Atkins framed the agenda as an effort to bring more crypto activity onshore by giving market participants clear and workable rules for capital formation, asset custody, and secondary market trading.

This represents a meaningful shift in posture from the SEC's prior enforcement first approach. Clearer custody rules in particular would unlock participation from registered investment advisers and institutional asset managers who have been sitting on the sidelines due to liability concerns around holding client crypto assets.

## Deep Dive

Deep Dive

## Lean Ethereum: Vitalik's Most Ambitious Roadmap Since The Merge

A detailed look at what post-quantum cryptography and protocol simplification mean for the network and everyone building on it

On July 4, 2026, Vitalik Buterin published what may be the single most consequential Ethereum strategic document since The Merge. He called it the Lean Ethereum roadmap and it outlines where the network is headed over the next three to four years across three main pillars: quantum resistance, native privacy, and scalable verification. The scope is enormous and the implications for developers, investors, and institutions are profound.

### Why Now

The timing is not accidental. Quantum computing has moved from theoretical threat to credible near-term risk for cryptographic systems. The specific algorithms that secure most of today's blockchain infrastructure including elliptic curve digital signature algorithms and BLS signature schemes are mathematically vulnerable to sufficiently powerful quantum computers. Cryptographers generally estimate that cryptographically relevant quantum computers could arrive within the next decade. Ethereum's current architecture does not survive that transition without a redesign.

At the same time privacy has become a genuine user need rather than a fringe concern. Most Ethereum transactions are fully public. That was acceptable when the network was primarily a developer playground. Now that banks, asset managers, healthcare companies, and governments are exploring on-chain infrastructure, the total transparency of a public blockchain creates real problems for institutional adoption. Vitalik named privacy a first class goal for the first time alongside quantum resistance and scalability which is a significant philosophical shift for the Ethereum Foundation.

### The Three Pillars

Quantum resistance is the most urgent thread. The roadmap calls for replacing BLS, KZG, and ECDSA cryptographic schemes with post-quantum alternatives. The specific approach centers on STARK based verification which relies on hash functions rather than elliptic curve math and is believed to be secure against quantum attacks. Work on quantum-safe blob designs is already underway and Buterin described the urgency level as high. The target is a network that supports scalable state of approximately 100 terabytes secured entirely by post-quantum cryptographic proofs by 2030.

"Quantum safety has shifted up a LOT in priority. Work on quantum-safe blob designs is already underway."\
— Vitalik Buterin, July 4, 2026

Privacy as a first class goal means Ethereum is planning native protocol level privacy rather than continuing to rely on third party mixers or Layer 2 privacy solutions. The specific mechanism has not been fully specified yet but the roadmap points toward privacy preserving transaction architectures that allow selective disclosure. A user or institution could prove that a transaction is valid without revealing the amounts, counterparties, or asset types involved. This is technically demanding but represents the kind of infrastructure that could unlock regulated institutional participation at scale.

Scalability and verification simplification form the third pillar. The roadmap proposes replacing the current model of direct transaction re-execution by all nodes with recursive STARK based verification. Instead of every node re-running every computation, nodes verify compact cryptographic proofs that the computation was done correctly. This dramatically reduces the computational burden of running a full node and opens the door to much higher transaction throughput without sacrificing decentralization.

### Architectural Changes

Beyond the three headline priorities the Lean Ethereum roadmap includes a cluster of architectural changes that collectively make the network simpler and more maintainable. The consensus mechanism is being redesigned toward one or two round finality which reduces the time between transaction submission and irreversibility. Multidimensional gas pricing would allow the network to price different computational resources independently rather than collapsing them into a single gas unit. New state architecture proposals target 100 terabytes of scalable state. Client architecture simplification through protocol cleanup aims to reduce the maintenance burden on the teams building Ethereum clients.

The cumulative effect of these changes makes Ethereum a substantially different protocol by 2029 than it is today. Buterin is careful to emphasize gradual rollout with minimal disruption to existing applications. DeFi protocols, NFT platforms, and Layer 2 networks built on today's Ethereum are not being abandoned. The migration path is intended to be smooth.

### What This Means for the Ecosystem

For developers the roadmap creates both opportunity and upgrade work. Applications that rely on specific cryptographic assumptions will need audits and potential rewrites as post-quantum primitives are introduced. Teams building privacy sensitive applications get a clear signal to build toward native protocol support rather than bolting on third party solutions. The shift to STARK based verification will eventually make zk-proof generation a core infrastructure skill across the ecosystem.

For investors the roadmap reinforces Ethereum's position as the highest quality smart contract infrastructure for institutional use cases. The combination of quantum resistance, native privacy, and massive scalability directly addresses the three objections that large financial institutions most frequently raise when evaluating on-chain deployment. BlackRock's BUIDL fund growth, the launch of Ethereum Institutional as an organization, and the record validator entry queue all suggest that the market is already pricing in this trajectory. Vitalik's roadmap gives that thesis a concrete technical foundation.

For regulators the Lean Ethereum roadmap is actually good news. A network with native privacy that supports selective disclosure rather than full anonymity is far easier to work with in a regulated context than either a fully transparent ledger or a fully opaque one. The post-quantum security goal also aligns with guidance from NIST and other standards bodies that have been pushing financial infrastructure providers toward post-quantum readiness.

### The Bottom Line

The Lean Ethereum roadmap is Ethereum's most ambitious technical agenda since The Merge and perhaps in the network's history. Quantum computers that threaten today's cryptography may still be years away but the preparation window to redesign a global financial network is measured in years not months. Vitalik is moving now. The DeFi ecosystem, institutional capital, and a growing regulatory framework are all moving in the same direction. The next three years on Ethereum will be defined by this roadmap and the teams building toward it.
