## 🌍 Geopolitical Jitters Drive Volatility — Then a Relief Rally

The week opened with significant crypto market turbulence as the escalating U.S.-Israel-Iran conflict spooked risk-asset holders. Bitcoin briefly slipped below \$67,000 before clawing back gains as a report surfaced suggesting the U.S. and Iran are discussing a 45-day ceasefire agreement.

📌 Takeaway: Crypto's "digital safe haven" narrative continues to be tested. Until geopolitical clarity arrives, expect elevated volatility around news cycle events.

## 🏦 Charles Schwab Launches "Schwab Crypto" — \$12 Trillion Gateway Opens

This week's most consequential institutional news: **Charles Schwab officially launched "Schwab Crypto,"** enabling direct spot trading of Bitcoin and Ethereum for clients across its 38.9 million active accounts and \$12.22 trillion in assets under management. The phased rollout begins with Schwab employees and early-access waitlist users in Q2 2026, expanding across the U.S. (excluding New York and Louisiana at launch).

Crucially, Schwab's offering provides *direct asset ownership* — not ETF exposure — operated through Charles Schwab Premier Bank. The brokerage is also a founding backer of EDX Markets, the institutional crypto exchange co-founded with Citadel Securities and Fidelity Digital Assets.

📌 Takeaway: This is a watershed moment. When one of America's largest retail brokerage platforms gives 38.9 million account holders a one-click path to self-custody Bitcoin, the "mainstream adoption" phase is no longer coming — it's here.

Ethereum

## 🔷 Ethereum Staking Surge & the Fusaka Hard Fork

The Ethereum Foundation moved closer to its 70,000 staked ETH target this week after transferring \$92.2 million in ETH to the Beacon Deposit Contract — continuing its treasury policy shift announced in February 2026 to start actively staking a portion of its holdings.

On the development side, Ethereum's **Fusaka hard fork** — already deployed — raised the block gas limit to 60 million and introduced Peer Data Availability Sampling (PeerDAS), designed to boost Layer-2 rollup data throughput by up to **8×**. A second scalability-focused hard fork is planned for later in 2026.

ETH is currently trading around \$1,970, roughly 60% below its August 2025 all-time high of \$4,946. Analysts are split: some see a floor forming; others project a further decline to the \$1,148 macro-support zone if broader risk sentiment continues to deteriorate.

📌 Takeaway: Ethereum's fundamentals (staking growth, L2 throughput) are strengthening even as price lags. Patient investors may see this as an accumulation window — but know your risk tolerance.

Regulation

## ⚖️ CLARITY Act Senate Markup Looms — Stablecoin Yield Deal Reached

U.S. crypto regulation is entering a critical window. The **CLARITY Act** — the landmark bill that would resolve the long-standing SEC vs. CFTC jurisdictional battle over digital assets — is headed for a Senate Banking Committee markup hearing expected in the second half of April 2026. A failure to advance before May could jeopardize passage ahead of the November midterms.

A significant breakthrough emerged: Senators Thom Tillis and Angela Alsobrooks reached an agreement in principle on the contentious **stablecoin yield provision**. The compromise would prohibit passive yield on static stablecoin balances (eliminating bank-like interest accounts) while allowing activity-linked incentives. Separately, full implementation of the GENIUS Act — governing stablecoin licensing — is targeted for July 18, 2026.

📌 Takeaway: The U.S. regulatory picture is crystallizing faster than most expected. If CLARITY passes, it would be the single most important piece of crypto legislation in American history — watch April's Senate hearings closely.

## ⚛️ Q-Day and Bitcoin: How Real Is the Quantum Threat — and What Happens Next?

A bombshell research paper from Google dropped at the end of March 2026 and the crypto community is still processing the implications. The paper suggests that breaking Bitcoin's elliptic-curve cryptography (ECDSA) may require **far fewer qubits than previously estimated** — potentially as few as 500,000 physical qubits, roughly a 20× reduction from the 2023 consensus estimate of ~10 million.

### What Google Found

Google's team modeled a real-time quantum attack scenario and concluded that an adversary with a sufficiently advanced fault-tolerant quantum computer could potentially crack an in-flight Bitcoin transaction — one where the public key is briefly exposed in the mempool — in approximately **9 minutes**, fast enough to beat the network's 10-minute block confirmation window roughly 41% of the time. Google has set an internal deadline of **2029** to migrate its own authentication infrastructure to post-quantum standards.

### The Taproot Wrinkle

Bitcoin's Taproot upgrade, adopted in 2021, improves privacy and scripting capabilities — but comes with a catch. Taproot exposes public keys by default, which quantum researchers say could widen the pool of **vulnerable wallets**. Estimates suggest up to 6.9 million BTC in addresses with exposed public keys could theoretically be at risk in a post-Q-Day world.

### The Response: BIP-360 and Ethereum's Head Start

The Bitcoin developer community has proposed **BIP-360**, a gradual migration path that would allow users to voluntarily move funds into post-quantum address types — avoiding the chaos of a forced, network-wide flag day. The approach is designed to be opt-in at first, then potentially mandatory after a long transition window.

Ethereum's developers, meanwhile, have already launched a more aggressive post-quantum migration roadmap, with Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin having outlined a detailed recovery fork procedure in the event quantum computers become capable of breaking current cryptography on short notice.

### The Dissenting View

Not everyone is sounding the alarm. **Ark Invest** published a note arguing that today's quantum computers — including Google's Willow chip — remain orders of magnitude too weak to threaten Bitcoin's cryptography, and that "Q-Day" for crypto is still at minimum a decade away under most realistic hardware scaling scenarios. The key caveat: quantum hardware has historically advanced faster than expert consensus predicted.

### What This Means for You and Your Clients

Think of it like this: Bitcoin's current cryptography is a padlock that only a person with extraordinary physical strength could break. Today's quantum computers are toddlers. But researchers just revised the estimate of how strong someone needs to be to break it — downward. They're not there yet, but the timeline is moving.

For most holders, this is not an imminent concern. But for institutional custodians, long-term HODLers, and protocol developers, it's time to ask hard questions about migration paths. The proactive move: avoid reusing addresses, consider wallets that don't expose public keys (pre-Taproot P2PKH addresses), and stay closely tuned to BIP-360 progress on the Bitcoin developer mailing list.

⚡ Bottom line: Q-Day is not tomorrow. But the Google paper moved the goalposts in a way that demands attention. This is the kind of systemic risk that takes years to play out — and years to fix. The builders who start now will be the ones who protect their clients later.
