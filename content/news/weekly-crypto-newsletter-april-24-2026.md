## Strategy Drops \$2.54B on Bitcoin — Now Holds More BTC Than BlackRock

INSTITUTIONAL · BITCOIN · TREASURY

Michael Saylor's Strategy made its **third-largest single Bitcoin purchase on record** this week — 34,164 BTC for approximately \$2.54 billion at an average price of \$74,395 per coin. The buy pushes Strategy's total holdings to **815,061 BTC**, a stash now worth roughly \$63 billion and one that overtakes BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (802,823 BTC) to make Strategy the largest single corporate holder of Bitcoin on earth.

The funding mechanism is notable: Strategy raised capital primarily through perpetual preferred stock (STRC) and new equity issuances rather than taking on fresh debt. CEO Saylor has stated the firm's ambition to ultimately raise **\$84 billion** in the medium-to-long term to continue accumulating. Meanwhile, Strategy's average cost basis sits at \$75,527 per coin — essentially at the money at current prices, which means the position is modestly in profit.

**Why it matters:** Strategy overtaking BlackRock is a symbolic inversion. The world's largest asset manager built its BTC position through a fund product serving retail and institutional clients. Strategy built its through a leveraged corporate treasury. Both now serve as price-support mechanisms for Bitcoin, but through entirely different capital structures and investor bases.

Bitcoin climbed past \$77,500 on the news, aided by geopolitical tailwinds (President Trump's ceasefire extension) and \$1.4 billion in weekly inflows to global crypto funds. Analysts are watching the critical \$80,000–\$82,000 resistance zone as the next key test for the remainder of Q2.

## April 2026 Becomes the Worst Month for Crypto Exploits Since February 2025

SECURITY · DEFI · HACKS

Crypto protocols have lost over **\$606 million to exploits in just 18 days** in April — making this the single worst month for hacks in over a year. The headline event is the **KelpDAO bridge exploit**, in which attackers linked to North Korea's Lazarus Group drained 116,500 rsETH (\$292–294 million) on April 18, making it the largest single DeFi theft of 2026.

What makes the KelpDAO hack technically distinct is that it **was not a smart contract vulnerability**. Attackers targeted the off-chain RPC nodes powering LayerZero's Decentralized Verifier Network, corrupting the bridge's ability to verify cross-chain state. Because the bridge held reserves backing rsETH across more than 20 networks simultaneously, the failure cascaded instantly — triggering market freezes by Aave, SparkLend, and Fluid, and wiping **\$13–14 billion in TVL** from DeFi protocols within 48 hours.

**Security lesson:** Cross-chain bridge infrastructure remains crypto's most dangerous attack surface — not because of bad smart contract code, but because of the off-chain oracle and validator layers that sit beneath it. The "security" of a bridge is only as strong as its weakest off-chain component.

The Arbitrum Security Council froze 30,766 ETH held on Arbitrum One in an emergency action, one of the first times a major chain's governance council used emergency powers to freeze hacker funds in real time. Regulators — particularly Jefferies analysts — are warning that this scale of exploit may force major banks reconsidering blockchain plans to pump the brakes.

04 / REGULATION

## 12 European Banks Form Qivalis — A MiCA-Compliant Euro Stablecoin Is Coming in H2 2026

REGULATION · STABLECOINS · EUROPE

A consortium of twelve major European banks — including BNP Paribas, BBVA, ING, UniCredit, SEB, Deutsche Bank's DZ BANK, and others — has formed **Qivalis**, a joint venture backed by Fireblocks, to launch a MiCA-compliant euro-denominated stablecoin. Target launch: H2 2026, pending approval from the Dutch central bank (De Nederlandsche Bank).

The timing is deliberate. The stablecoin market hit **\$305 billion in January 2026**, but **99% remains dollar-denominated**. Euro-pegged stablecoins account for roughly \$650 million — less than 0.3% of the total market. Qivalis is explicitly designed to challenge dollar stablecoin dominance in institutional European payment flows, using Fireblocks for tokenization infrastructure, wallet custody, and MiCA-compliant compliance tooling.

**Consulting angle:** If you work with European clients or operate cross-border, this is worth tracking closely. A fully-regulated euro stablecoin backed by 12 TradFi institutions changes the settlement calculus for European B2B crypto applications — especially as the GENIUS Act continues to cement US dollar stablecoin rails domestically.

05 / POLICY

## 100+ Crypto Firms Press the Senate to Mark Up the CLARITY Act — "We Need a Framework Now"

REGULATION · POLICY · UNITED STATES

A coalition of **over 100 crypto companies** — including Coinbase and Ripple — filed a public letter this week urging the Senate Banking Committee to immediately mark up the **CLARITY Act**, the federal market structure bill that would establish clear rules for digital asset classification, trading venues, and custody. A Senate Banking Committee official signaled the bill could clear committee before the end of April.

Separately, SEC Chair Paul Atkins announced that the agency's long-awaited "Regulation Crypto" proposal — which attempts to define which digital assets fall outside securities law — has been forwarded to the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs. The SEC also plans to release its innovation exemption soon. Meanwhile, Russia this week passed legislation **allowing businesses to use crypto for cross-border trade settlements**, even under active sanctions — a significant escalation in state-level crypto adoption outside the Western system.

**Reading the tea leaves:** The regulatory environment is moving fast on multiple fronts simultaneously. The GENIUS Act operationalizing stablecoin rules, the CLARITY Act defining market structure, SEC's Regulation Crypto, and Japan classifying crypto as a financial product all signal that the window for regulatory arbitrage is closing — and the window for compliant, institutional-grade products is opening.

## The Bitcoin Treasury Reckoning:\
Why the mNAV Premium Era Is Over — And What Comes Next

Original analysis · 35+ sources researched · April 24, 2026

Something unusual is happening in the Bitcoin treasury space this week. Strategy just made its third-largest BTC purchase in history — \$2.54 billion in a single week — while everywhere else in the corporate Bitcoin treasury ecosystem, the buying has essentially stopped. Non-Strategy treasury companies purchased a combined **~1,000 BTC in the last 30 days**. That's a **98.6% collapse** from the August 2025 peak of 69,000 BTC per month. The number of active corporate buyers has dropped by 76%.

To understand what's happening — and what it means — you need to understand the original playbook. And then you need to understand why the playbook only still works for one company.

### The Original Playbook: The mNAV Flywheel

When Michael Saylor made MicroStrategy the world's first Bitcoin treasury company in August 2020, the actual strategy was more financially sophisticated than "buy Bitcoin and hold." The core insight was this: **if equity markets value BTC held at the corporate level at a premium to spot Bitcoin prices, you can arbitrage that premium to buy more Bitcoin.**

Here's how the flywheel worked in practice:

Step 1: Hold Bitcoin on your balance sheet. Step 2: The market assigns a premium to your stock versus NAV (Net Asset Value of BTC held) — because investors who can't easily buy BTC directly will pay a premium for BTC-correlated equity exposure. Step 3: Issue new equity at that premium. Step 4: Use the proceeds to buy more Bitcoin. Step 5: More BTC raises NAV. Step 6: Strong BTC performance re-expands the equity premium. Step 7: Repeat at scale.

This is the mNAV (market-to-NAV) flywheel. When the premium (mNAV ratio) is above 1.0x, every dollar of equity issuance buys more than one dollar of Bitcoin per share — it's accretive. The machine generates its own fuel.

The problem is that this flywheel requires two things to be true simultaneously: (1) Bitcoin must be appreciating, and (2) equity markets must continue to assign a premium to BTC held at the corporate level. In 2026, condition #2 has broken down for all but the largest players.

Key finding: A 10% drop in mNAV wipes more equity value than a 10% drop in BTC price. The mNAV multiple is the more fragile variable.

The Grayscale Bitcoin Adopters ETF was created to offer diversified exposure to this universe. ### The Proprietary Framing: Amazon vs. eToys

Here is the key insight most crypto commentary is missing: **The corporate Bitcoin treasury wave has entered its "dot-com shakeout" phase — and the outcome is the same as 1999–2001.** Not that the technology (Bitcoin) loses. But that most of the companies that tried to ride it as a financial gimmick are collapsing, while the one or two entities with structural advantages consolidate the space.

Think of it this way: In the late 1990s, "being on the internet" was a real and durable advantage. Amazon was real. But so were dozens of companies that simply added ".com" to their name and watched their stock triple. When the multiple compression came, the hollowness of the copycat plays became obvious — not because the internet wasn't real, but because the equity premium evaporated.

The same dynamic is playing out in Bitcoin treasury. **Strategy is Amazon. The copycat treasury companies are the eToys and Pets.coms.**

Strategy survives because of three structural advantages that most copycats lack: (1) **Scale** — at 815,000 BTC, it is the price of Bitcoin, not a bet on Bitcoin. Institutions buying Bitcoin exposure have no better proxy. (2) **Capital access** — Saylor's ability to issue preferred stock and convertible notes at scale means the equity machine keeps running even at compressed mNAV ratios. (3) **Brand** — Strategy is the canonical BTC treasury vehicle. The Grayscale Bitcoin Adopters ETF, index inclusion, and institutional coverage all flow to it first.

💡 Key Insight — The Bifurcation Test

The question to ask about any Bitcoin treasury company isn't "how much BTC do they hold?" It's **"can they continue to issue equity or debt at a non-dilutive rate when mNAV is at or below 1.0x?"** If yes — they survive the shakeout. If no — they're a zombie: BTC on the balance sheet, no mechanism to accumulate more, and a stock price in terminal decline. The mNAV premium era is over for the copycats. It may be contracting even for Strategy if BTC stalls. But the underlying thesis — that corporate BTC treasury is a legitimate asset class — is now institutionally validated by MSCI inclusion, spot ETF flows, and the US Strategic Bitcoin Reserve.

### What to Watch Going Forward

**1. The mNAV floor test:** If Bitcoin pulls back toward \$65,000 and Strategy's mNAV drops toward 1.0x, it will test whether the equity machine truly keeps running at parity. A sub-1.0x mNAV would be the most significant stress test the model has ever faced.

**2. The zombie liquidations:** Galaxy's warning that 5+ treasury firms face forced sales is a leading indicator. Each forced liquidation is a mini-supply event for Bitcoin and a datapoint that regulators and institutions will use to calibrate risk. Watch for the pattern of which companies survive (operating revenue + BTC) vs. which don't (pure holding vehicles).

**3. US Strategic Bitcoin Reserve activation:** The Trump executive order establishing the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve was signed in March 2025, but Congress has not yet appropriated new buying. If and when legislative authority is granted to actively purchase Bitcoin for the reserve, it would be the single largest structural demand event in the asset's history — and would validate every corporate treasury that has been quietly accumulating.

**4. The next MSCI review:** MSCI reversed its exclusion decision in January 2026 but signaled it would revisit the broader question of how to classify non-operating companies that hold digital assets. A future exclusion would force \$10–15B in passive fund rebalancing — a significant tail risk for the surviving treasury companies.
