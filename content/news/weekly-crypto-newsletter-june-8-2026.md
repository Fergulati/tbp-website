## DEEP RESEARCH FEATURE

## The GENIUS Act: How America's Stablecoin Law Is Reshaping the Global Financial Order

*A deep research analysis by Fergulati Finds*

---

### What Is the GENIUS Act

The Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for US Stablecoins Act was signed into law by President Trump on July 18, 2025. It is the first federal framework ever enacted for US dollar stablecoins. The law fundamentally restructures who can issue digital dollars and on what terms. Prior to this legislation the stablecoin market operated in a regulatory gray zone that allowed offshore issuers and lightly audited reserve structures to dominate.

The total stablecoin market now stands at \$322 billion exceeding the foreign exchange reserves of 95 nations including developed economies like the United Kingdom and Canada. Annual stablecoin transfer volumes reached \$18.4 trillion in 2025 surpassing both Visa at \$15.7 trillion and Mastercard at \$9.8 trillion. The GENIUS Act governs a market that is no longer a niche digital asset experiment. It is now critical financial infrastructure.

---

### The Law's Core Provisions

Only three categories of entities may issue payment stablecoins under the GENIUS Act. Category one is subsidiaries of insured depository institutions. Category two is entities holding a federal license from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency. Category three is entities licensed under a state regime that the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee has certified as substantially similar to federal standards.

All permitted issuers must maintain 1:1 reserves in highly liquid low risk assets. Eligible reserve assets include US currency and demand deposits at insured institutions and Treasury securities maturing in 93 days or less and overnight repurchase agreements backed by short term Treasuries. Monthly reserve reports are required and must be verified by third party audits. CEOs and CFOs must personally certify accuracy under penalty of perjury. Yield payments to stablecoin holders are explicitly prohibited for payment stablecoins. Algorithmic stablecoins that lack 1:1 asset backing are effectively banned from US markets. Unauthorized issuers face criminal penalties of up to \$1 million per day.

State qualified issuers with less than \$10 billion in outstanding stablecoins may operate under certified state regimes. Crossing \$10 billion triggers mandatory transition to joint federal and state oversight with a primary federal banking regulator assuming supervisory responsibility. Large technology companies face an additional hurdle. Any public company not predominantly engaged in financial activities must receive unanimous approval from the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee to issue stablecoins. The law also strips the SEC and CFTC of jurisdiction over payment stablecoins by clarifying they are neither securities nor commodities.

---

### The Regulatory Timeline

The GENIUS Act was signed July 18, 2025. All primary federal regulators must issue implementing regulations no later than July 18, 2026. The OCC published a proposed rule in February 2026. Treasury and the FDIC published their own proposed rules in early 2026. The Bank Secrecy Act and OFAC related rules are on a separate track with the public comment period closing June 9, 2026. The law's operative requirements for stablecoin issuers take effect on the earlier of 18 months from enactment which is January 18, 2027 or 120 days after regulators finalize their rules. That final rulemaking deadline is now six weeks away creating significant pressure on agencies to complete their work.

### Winners: Who Benefits From the New Framework

**Circle and USDC**

Circle is the clearest winner produced by the GENIUS Act. The company behind the \$64.5 billion USDC stablecoin spent nearly a decade building compliance infrastructure and lobbying for exactly this kind of legislation. USDC already meets most GENIUS Act requirements. Circle can now compete on a playing field where its years of regulatory investment become a competitive moat rather than an unnecessary cost.

**Major US Banks**

JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America and Wells Fargo are positioned to become dominant forces in stablecoin issuance. Bank subsidiaries can issue stablecoins with minimal additional capital requirements while leveraging existing regulatory relationships and Federal Reserve account access and consumer trust built over decades. Banks also retain the unique ability to issue interest bearing tokenized deposits which are not subject to the yield prohibition applied to payment stablecoins. This gives banks a structural product advantage over crypto native competitors.

**Custody and Infrastructure Providers**

Every new GENIUS Act compliant issuer requires regulated custody services. Coinbase Custody and BitGo and Anchorage Digital are positioned to capture significant growth as issuance scales. The compliance infrastructure buildout itself creates a multiyear revenue opportunity for firms already operating in this space.

**PayPal and Established Fintech Players**

PayPal's PYUSD stablecoin and its established compliance operations make it well positioned for the post GENIUS Act market. Fiserv signed agreements with PayPal and Circle immediately after the bill passed signaling that traditional payment infrastructure is actively aligning with the new framework.

---

### Losers: Who Faces the Steepest Challenges

**Tether**

Tether's \$169 billion USDT market cap is the largest in stablecoins and built on an offshore structure. Years of resisting full audits and holding reserve assets that exceed GENIUS Act limits present major compliance gaps. Tether responded in late 2025 by announcing USA₮ which is a purpose built US compliant stablecoin designed to operate within GENIUS Act requirements. The strategy keeps Tether's international USDT operations outside US jurisdiction while creating a compliant product for the US market. Whether USA₮ can capture meaningful market share against Circle's entrenched compliance brand remains the critical question.

**Algorithmic and Yield Bearing Stablecoin Protocols**

Algorithmic stablecoins are structurally incompatible with the 1:1 reserve requirement. They have no path to compliance and no US market access. Yield bearing stablecoins are excluded from the category of payment stablecoins meaning DeFi protocols that rely on yield from idle stablecoin deposits face structural revenue pressure. The Senate Banking Committee's companion CLARITY Act framework reinforces this by prohibiting passive yield on stablecoins while allowing yield tied to specific on chain activity.

### The Global Geopolitical Race

The GENIUS Act has triggered reactions at the highest levels of global financial governance.

**China**

Chinese state media called for yuan backed stablecoins "sooner rather than later" in the immediate aftermath of the Senate vote. China's e-CNY government issued digital currency has recorded \$7.3 trillion in cumulative transactions but public adoption within China remains low with familiar payment platforms like Alipay and WeChat Pay still accounting for 90% of mobile payments. Hong Kong's May 2025 Stablecoins Ordinance creates a regional template that permits stablecoin issuance referencing the Hong Kong dollar and other major currencies.

**European Union**

ECB President Lagarde testified before the European Parliament that dollar backed stablecoins create risks for monetary policy and financial stability. The ECB views digital dollarization through stablecoins as a direct threat to the euro's monetary sovereignty and to the effectiveness of ECB rate transmission across the eurozone. Legislative initiatives to authorize a digital euro have stalled in part because it is seen as competing with bank deposits. The EU is now exploring recognition of non euro stablecoins under updated MiCAR rules which would represent a significant reversal of the de facto ban currently in place.

**The Broader Global Response**

As of mid 2025, 49 governments had launched formal CBDC pilots. 11 countries had already launched CBDCs. The Atlantic Council's CBDC Tracker notes that many of these projects are directly accelerating in response to US stablecoin policy momentum. The United States is entering this competition from a position of structural advantage. Dollar backed stablecoins are already dominant at scale. Tether and Circle are the third largest purchasers of US Treasury bills behind only JPMorgan and China. Growth projections estimate the stablecoin market could reach \$2 trillion by 2030 under the GENIUS Act framework which would create a self reinforcing demand cycle for US Treasury securities and entrench dollar dominance in digital payments.

### What This Means for Crypto Consulting Clients

The GENIUS Act is not a compliance checkbox story. It is a structural realignment of global financial infrastructure. Clients operating in or adjacent to the stablecoin market need to assess their positioning across four dimensions.

First: any business that accepts or holds stablecoins must verify whether their counterparty stablecoins will meet GENIUS Act standards before January 2027. Holding an unauthorized stablecoin after that date creates legal and operational risk.

Second: DeFi protocols operating on yield models must assess their exposure to yield bearing stablecoins that will lose US market access. The DeFi yield compression trend is already underway as USDC on Aave yields 2.61% against 3.14% at traditional brokerages.

Third: banks that have not yet explored stablecoin subsidiary strategies are falling behind competitors who are already building. The window to establish first mover positioning in compliant stablecoin infrastructure is closing.

Fourth: the global CBDC and foreign stablecoin response is worth monitoring. Clients with international payment operations need contingency plans for a future where competing dollar and yuan and euro digital currency rails create fragmented settlement environments.
