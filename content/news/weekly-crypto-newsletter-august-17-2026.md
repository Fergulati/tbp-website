## 5 Topics You Need to Know

### 1. SEC Punts on "Regulation Crypto" Vote

The SEC was scheduled to vote August 14 on a landmark proposal called Regulation Crypto. The vote would have launched the first formal crypto rulemaking under Chairman Paul Atkins. The meeting was canceled at the last minute due to what an SEC spokesperson called an unforeseen scheduling issue. The proposal would create an offering regime that lets qualifying crypto projects raise capital without triggering automatic SEC registration requirements. The cancellation leaves the industry without a clear timeline for formal rule adoption.

### 2. CLARITY Act Stalls in Senate

The Senate left for August recess without holding a vote on the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act known as the CLARITY Act. The bill was designed to resolve the longstanding question of which crypto assets fall under SEC versus CFTC jurisdiction. A White House meeting with executives from Coinbase and Ripple and Kraken was scheduled for August 19 and traders are watching it for any signal of executive branch pressure to revive the legislation.

### 3. Ethereum Staking Hits Record and EIP-8363 Faces Backlash

Staking now represents 34.4% of all Ethereum supply as of August 12. Italy's largest bank tripled its staked ETH ETF holdings to \$7.1 million. BNY partnered with Galaxy to integrate staking directly into institutional custody infrastructure. At the same time Ethereum's EIP-8363 staking proposal is facing backlash from critics who warn it poses risks to decentralization and DeFi stability. The total value locked across Ethereum's DeFi ecosystem reached \$41.84 billion as of August 8 which is a 7.82% gain over the trailing 30 days.

### 4. Ether.fi Adds Tokenized Stocks and Portfolio Loans

DeFi platform Ether.fi announced that users can now trade tokenized stocks and metals and crypto assets directly through its app. The integrated market uses the Aave lending protocol on Optimism and allows users to borrow against their portfolios without selling their positions. This is a meaningful step toward bridging traditional brokerage functionality with onchain DeFi rails and is worth monitoring as a product model other protocols may follow.

### 5. GENIUS Act Rulemaking Is Underway

The GENIUS Act was signed into law in July 2025 and established the first federal framework for USD backed payment stablecoins. Issuers must maintain 1:1 reserves in cash or short term Treasurys and disclose reserves monthly. As of August 2026 the OCC and the Federal Reserve and the Treasury Department are all engaged in formal rulemaking to implement the law. New anti-money laundering rules were published in July 2026. Stablecoin issuers and the platforms that list them are watching closely as these rules will determine compliance burdens for years ahead.

## Deep Dive: Real World Asset Tokenization Is No Longer Experimental

**The shift from pilot to infrastructure**

Real world asset tokenization (RWA) is the process of representing ownership of physical or financial assets as digital tokens on a blockchain. Think of it as putting the deed to a property or a Treasury bond into a smart contract so it can be transferred and fractionalized and settled at the speed of a blockchain transaction. What was a niche experiment two years ago is now one of the fastest growing sectors in crypto.

**The numbers tell the story**

The onchain RWA market crossed \$37.5 billion in total market capitalization in 2026. That represents 100% growth year over year. The market sat at roughly \$5.5 billion in early 2025. Tokenized US Treasurys account for approximately \$12.88 billion of that total. Private credit accounts for about 61% of all tokenized asset categories. BlackRock's BUIDL tokenized money market fund has grown past \$25 billion in AUM alone. Boston Consulting Group projects this market will reach \$16 trillion by 2030. Standard Chartered projects \$30 trillion by 2034.

**Who is building**

BlackRock and JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs and Franklin Templeton are all actively launching tokenized products. JPMorgan's Kinexys platform now enables real time cross border settlement of tokenized Treasurys in under five seconds. The US is the current global leader in institutional RWA adoption driven by early regulatory clarity from the SEC and the entry of major custodians into the space. The EU completed its MiCA framework transition which opened additional pathways for tokenized instruments in European markets.

**The regulatory foundation is forming**

In January 2026 the SEC issued a joint statement confirming that tokenized securities are subject to existing federal securities laws. The EU's MiCA transition is now complete. The Cayman Islands wrote tokenized funds directly into statute. The industry also converged on a neutral token standard called ERC-7943 which is designed to make tokenized assets more portable across platforms.

**What is not working yet**

The promise of tokenization collides with a structural problem: liquidity is fragmented. Of the thousands of tokenized assets tracked across blockchains 910 of them recorded zero weekly transfers as of mid-2026. 62 assets hold 88% of the total value. Five products account for roughly half of the market. Tokenized assets issued on one blockchain often cannot move to another without losing secondary market depth. Regulatory fragmentation across jurisdictions compounds the problem because an asset that is legal to hold in Singapore may be inaccessible to US investors.

**Why this matters for your portfolio and your clients**

RWA tokenization is not a speculative bet on a new coin. It is the rearchitecting of how traditional financial instruments are issued and transferred and settled. The clients who understand this shift earliest are the ones positioned to access tokenized private credit and Treasury yield products before they become mainstream. The consulting question is no longer whether to pay attention to RWA. The question is which platforms and which asset classes carry the least regulatory and liquidity risk right now.
