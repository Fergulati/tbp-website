Bitcoin endured one of its ugliest stretches in months before finding its footing heading into this week. The world's leading cryptocurrency dropped below \$70,000 and briefly touched the \$61,000 zone as exchange-traded fund outflows accelerated and geopolitical tensions pushed investors toward defensive positioning across risk markets. A 4 percent rally on Sunday June 7 sparked renewed optimism as buyers returned with conviction. Strategy chairman Michael Saylor hinted at a resumption of his company's Bitcoin accumulation strategy and that announcement helped stabilize broader market sentiment heading into the new week.

The recovery arrives at a critical moment. Investors are now watching closely to see whether the June 5 end of the record outflow streak marks a genuine turning point or merely a brief pause in a broader period of institutional repositioning.

Big Story

## Record Bitcoin ETF Outflows: What Happened and Why It Matters

Between May 15 and June 3 2026 U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded 13 consecutive trading days of net outflows totaling \$4.33 billion. That streak is the longest and most severe since spot Bitcoin ETFs first launched in January 2024. Over those 13 days approximately 59,400 BTC left ETF custody. Total net assets across all U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs fell from \$104.29 billion on May 15 down to \$82.83 billion by June 3. BlackRock's IBIT fund alone accounted for roughly \$3.3 billion of that total outflow.

Three forces converged at precisely the same moment to trigger this historic event. To begin with the Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate steady at 3.50 to 3.75 percent and stripped language from its policy statement that previously indicated progress toward its 2 percent inflation target. Futures markets responded quickly and priced in a 68.8 percent probability that the Fed would deliver zero cuts for the entire year of 2026. Furthermore the 30-year Treasury yield surged to 5.197 percent on May 20 reaching its highest level since July 2007. When bond yields climb that sharply the opportunity cost of holding an asset without yield becomes impossible to ignore for most institutional investors. In addition to those monetary pressures escalating tensions involving Iran intensified defensive positioning across risk markets at precisely the wrong moment.

The streak ended on June 5 with a slim \$3.05 million in net inflows. BlackRock's IBIT led that recovery with \$47.66 million flowing in on that single day. Importantly even after this historic outflow event cumulative lifetime net inflows into U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs since their January 2024 launch still exceed \$55 billion. The stress test was real but the structural position built since ETF launch remains largely intact.

Industry News

## The Former FTX Founder Files a Formal Presidential Pardon Request

The former FTX founder who is serving a 25 year sentence following his conviction for one of the largest financial frauds in history has formally submitted a pardon request to President Donald Trump. The application seeks a pardon after completion of sentence which would not erase his conviction but would restore certain civil liberties once he completes his prison term. Trump stated in January that he did not plan to issue the pardon and there is no indication that position has changed.

The development continues to draw attention to the regulatory and accountability questions still surrounding the 2022 FTX collapse. It is also a reminder that the crypto industry's credibility with institutional investors depends in no small part on how the legal system treats cases of this magnitude.

Regulation Watch

## New SEC Priorities and California's Digital Asset Licensing Deadline Approach

The regulatory landscape in the United States continued to evolve meaningfully this week. The SEC published its Draft Strategic Plan for fiscal years 2026 through 2030 designating digital assets and distributed ledger technology as the agency's first regulatory objective. That is the clearest signal yet that crypto oversight sits at the top of the SEC's institutional agenda going forward. Additionally the CFTC issued guidance approving a framework for cryptoasset perpetual futures contracts. That product class had until now developed almost entirely on offshore venues due to regulatory uncertainty and the new guidance opens the door to bringing that activity onshore under federal oversight.

On the state level California is preparing to enforce its Digital Financial Assets Law beginning July 1 2026. The law requires anyone engaging in digital financial asset business activity with a California resident to obtain a license from the state's Department of Financial Protection and Innovation. For anyone operating or advising in the crypto space with California clients this deadline is less than 30 days away and warrants immediate attention.

Sector Spotlight

## Stablecoins Are Quietly Becoming the Most Important Infrastructure in Crypto

The stablecoin market continued to demonstrate extraordinary growth heading into mid-2026. The global supply of fiat-backed stablecoins has surpassed \$273 billion and total settlement volume across stablecoin networks reached \$33 trillion in 2025. That figure surpassed Visa's annual throughput for the first time in history. Real-world payment volume using stablecoins doubled in 2025 to \$400 billion with an estimated 60 percent of that activity coming from B2B transactions.

Yield-bearing stablecoins are emerging as the fastest-growing segment in the space. Their value proposition combines stability with native yield in a single product and their supply has roughly doubled over the past year. Moreover stablecoin transaction volume grew 91 percent in 2025 to \$10.9 trillion putting it in direct competition with Visa's \$14.2 trillion in annual payments volume. As enterprises increasingly pursue real-world applications for stablecoin technology the adoption curve appears to be accelerating rather than plateauing.

Deep Dive

## What the 2026 Bitcoin ETF Crisis Reveals About Institutional Adoption

The record Bitcoin ETF outflows of May and June 2026 have prompted a fundamental question from analysts across Wall Street. Is institutional Bitcoin adoption reversing or simply pausing? After reviewing data from more than 30 sources including SEC 13F filings and independent analysis from CoinShares as well as reports from Grayscale and VanEck the evidence points strongly toward a pause rather than a structural retreat. The full picture is considerably more nuanced than any single headline suggests.

### The Macro Trigger: Three Shocks at Once

The dominant driver behind the outflows is a clear macroeconomic shock rather than a change in institutional conviction about Bitcoin itself. Sticky inflation at 3.8 percent CPI and 6 percent PPI in April 2026 eliminated any realistic expectation of Federal Reserve rate cuts this year. Rising Treasury yields then made the case for holding Bitcoin considerably harder to justify on a near-term basis. The 30-year yield reaching 5.197 percent on May 20 was the highest reading since July 2007 and it triggered forced unwinding of leveraged ETF positions across the market.

Beyond the yield pressure Bitcoin failed to serve as a safe haven during the Iran geopolitical episode. Gold surged to a record high of \$5,589 per ounce in January 2026 while Bitcoin sold off sharply. The two assets hit a historic correlation low of negative 0.88 in early 2026 meaning they moved almost perfectly in opposite directions. That performance gap cracked one of the core institutional narratives for holding Bitcoin as part of a diversified portfolio.

### Who Sold and Who Bought

The Q1 2026 13F filings which institutional investors must submit to the SEC reveal a striking bifurcation between investor types. CoinShares analyst Matthew Kimmell studied 1,813 reporting entities and found that hedge funds and brokerages drove virtually all of the selling during the quarter.

| Institution Type | Q1 2026 Holdings | Quarterly Change | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Investment Advisors | 150,300 BTC | 9,400 BTC reduction | Trimmed |
| Hedge Funds | 48,600 BTC | 31,400 BTC reduction | Aggressive Sell |
| Brokerages | 17,000 BTC | 18,800 BTC reduction | Aggressive Sell |
| Banks | 15,200 BTC | 7,700 BTC increase | Accumulated |
| Government / Sovereign | 8,300 BTC | 1,100 BTC increase | Accumulated |
| Private Equity | 6,500 BTC | 1,300 BTC increase | Accumulated |

Hedge funds and brokerages together accounted for 95 percent of all professional selling during Q1. Meanwhile a fundamentally different class of institution was buying into the drawdown. JPMorgan added 3,000 BTC worth of ETF exposure. Wells Fargo added 4,000 BTC. Citigroup disclosed 97 BTC worth of exposure in its 13F filing which marked the bank's first Bitcoin ETF position in any regulatory disclosure. Furthermore the Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth fund Mubadala acquired 1,100 BTC worth of ETF shares during Q1 making it the first confirmed sovereign wealth fund to enter U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs directly. The pattern is unmistakable. Short duration tactical players sold. Long duration strategic players accumulated through the drawdown.

### The AI Rotation

Strategy chairman Michael Saylor offered the clearest explanation for where much of the institutional capital went. He described a \$400 billion rotation into AI infrastructure over six months. Data centers and GPU buildouts and cloud infrastructure attracted capital that might otherwise have remained in Bitcoin positions. When AI equities offer earnings visibility and a compelling growth narrative they compete directly for the same institutional dollars that Bitcoin attracts. That dynamic played a meaningful role in the 2026 outflow story and represents a new form of competition that the crypto space had not previously faced at this scale.

### The Long Term Picture Remains Intact

Cumulative lifetime inflows into U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs still exceed \$55 billion as of early June 2026. The \$4.33 billion that left during the record streak represented roughly 7.5 percent of total lifetime inflows. For comparison when the SPDR Gold ETF launched in 2004 it lost 40 percent of its initial assets within its first few years. Bitcoin ETF holders are proving considerably more committed than early gold ETF holders were.

Furthermore less than 0.5 percent of U.S. advised wealth is currently allocated to crypto according to Grayscale Research. That figure means institutional adoption is genuinely in its earliest stages despite the high-profile ETF launches two and a half years ago. As more registered investment advisors complete their due diligence and incorporate crypto into model portfolios the structural inflow pressure should resume. IBIT alone raised \$24.7 billion in 2025 during a year when it posted negative returns. That performance ranked it sixth among all U.S. ETFs across every asset class showing that the holder base is structural and conviction-driven rather than purely momentum-based.

In addition regulatory clarity continues to accelerate. The GENIUS Act covering stablecoins has passed. The Clarity Act covering broader crypto market structure is expected to follow in 2026. Each piece of legislation removes barriers that previously kept large institutional allocators on the sidelines entirely.

### What the Analysts Expect

VanEck's Head of Digital Assets Research Matthew Sigel published a formal institutional analysis projecting Bitcoin at \$2.9 million by 2050 in his base case scenario. That implies a 15 percent compound annual growth rate from current levels. Bernstein projects Bitcoin reaching \$200,000 by 2027. Citigroup maintains a target range for the end of 2026 of \$143,000 to \$189,000. Tom Lee of Fundstrat sees a potential high of \$250,000 for 2026. Arthur Hayes of BitMEX expects Bitcoin to reach \$125,000 by December 2026.

Those projections all assume the macro headwinds of 2026 are cyclical rather than structural in nature. If the Federal Reserve eventually pivots toward lower rates and Treasury yields begin to fall the opportunity cost of holding Bitcoin without yield decreases sharply. Historical patterns suggest that when monetary conditions ease Bitcoin tends to benefit disproportionately relative to traditional assets.

The record outflows of May and June 2026 tell the story of tactical players responding to near-term macro pain. The simultaneous buying behavior of banks and sovereign wealth funds during that same period tells a different and arguably more important story about where long-duration institutional conviction actually resides as we move through 2026.
