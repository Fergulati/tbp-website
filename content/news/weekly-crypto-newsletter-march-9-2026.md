## Bitcoin Fights to Hold \$65K–\$70K Range Amid Bear Market Conditions

Bitcoin kicked off March with a **5% short-covering rally** on March 2, briefly pushing above **\$69,000**, before settling around **\$67,340** by week's end. The move was driven largely by leveraged positioning unwinding rather than fresh institutional buying, analysts warn.

The broader picture remains challenging: BTC is down **23% year-to-date**, extending a downturn that began last October. The Fear & Greed Index has sat in *Extreme Fear* territory for roughly three weeks. Polymarket prediction markets put the odds of Bitcoin falling below \$55,000 at some point in 2026 at **75%**.

That said, bearish sentiment is creating what some analysts are calling an accumulation zone. **Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth firms** Mubadala Investment Company and Al Warda Investments both added spot Bitcoin ETF exposure in mid-February, signaling that deep-pocketed institutions are using the dip to build positions.

## Crypto Industry Gains Access to Federal Reserve Payment System

In a landmark development, the **cryptocurrency industry gained its first access to the Federal Reserve payment system on March 5, 2026.** This integration positions digital assets closer to the core of the U.S. financial infrastructure, potentially enabling faster settlement, reduced counterparty risk, and increased legitimacy for blockchain-based financial services.

The move comes as the broader digital asset market cap sits at roughly **\$2.3 trillion**, with Bitcoin alone accounting for approximately **\$1.3 trillion**. Spot Bitcoin ETFs, now holding **\$88 billion worth of BTC** — about 6% of total supply — have become the primary institutional on-ramp.

Meanwhile, **XRP led altcoin inflows** at \$33.4 million in the latest weekly fund flows report from CoinShares, a sign that institutional capital is selectively rotating toward assets with greater regulatory clarity.

## The CLARITY Act: America's Crypto Rulebook Inches Forward in the Senate

The **Digital Asset Market Clarity Act of 2025** — passed by the House in July 2025 with a strong bipartisan vote of **294 to 134** — is now navigating a more complicated path through the Senate. Two Senate committees (Banking and Agriculture) have released separate drafts that must be reconciled before a full floor vote can happen.

The biggest flashpoint: a provision in the Senate Banking Committee draft that would **ban stablecoins from offering yield or interest** to holders. Coinbase threatened to withdraw its support over this clause, forcing the White House to convene emergency meetings between banking and crypto industry representatives — urging a compromise before March 1.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune has reportedly promised floor time this spring if both committees advance their bills. The legislation would grant the **CFTC exclusive jurisdiction** over digital commodity spot markets, mandate **1:1 reserves** for stablecoin issuers, and create a dual federal/state regulatory track. Its passage would mark the most significant U.S. crypto regulatory framework in history.

Key to watch: Whether the Senate Banking Committee resumes markup sessions in March — a development that could either accelerate or derail final passage before midterm elections.

## 2026's Big Blockchain Themes: Interoperability, Tokenization, and the DeFi Reboot

Three major narratives are shaping blockchain development heading into Q2 2026:

- **Interoperability**: Cross-chain bridges and multichain wallets are accelerating the push to connect siloed blockchain ecosystems. Developers predict 2026 will be the year the fragmented L2 landscape begins stitching back together into a "lightning-fast, cost-efficient, and truly interoperable" experience for users.
- **Real-World Asset (RWA) Tokenization**: From real estate to container ships to private equity, tokenization is moving from pilot programs to live deployments. Blockchain-as-a-Service (BaaS) providers are making enterprise adoption less technically daunting.
- **DeFi Reboot**: Ethereum and Solana are jointly setting the stage for a DeFi resurgence. Ethereum's Layer-2 ecosystem has matured significantly, while Solana's high throughput continues to attract developer talent. Grayscale's 2026 outlook predicts that "bipartisan crypto market structure legislation" will become U.S. law this year, bringing deeper on-chain integration with traditional finance.

On the conference calendar: the **DC Blockchain Summit** (March 17–18, Washington D.C.) brings policymakers and builders together to discuss regulation and Web3 infrastructure. Also notable: **SWIFT announced** it is working with 30+ financial institutions on a shared blockchain ledger for real-time cross-border payments.

## The 20 Millionth Bitcoin: What History's Most Significant Mining Milestone Means for Scarcity, Miners, and You

Sometime between **March 11–15, 2026**, the Bitcoin network will mine its **20 millionth coin** — crossing the threshold where **95% of all Bitcoin that will ever exist** is already in circulation. It's a milestone baked into Bitcoin's code since Satoshi Nakamoto published the whitepaper in 2008, and it's arriving right now.

It took roughly **17 years** to mine the first 20 million coins. The remaining ~1 million will take **over a century**, thanks to the halvings that progressively cut the block reward in half roughly every four years. (Due to rounding in Bitcoin's code, the true maximum supply is **20,999,999.9769 BTC** — not quite a clean 21 million.)

**The scarcity argument deepens.** Research from Chainalysis and River Financial estimates that between **2.3 and 3.7 million BTC are permanently lost** — trapped in wallets with forgotten passwords, destroyed hardware, and the estates of deceased holders who never shared their keys. Layer on the roughly **13 million "illiquid" BTC** held by long-term investors and institutions who rarely sell, and the supply available on exchanges sits at just about **3 million coins**. Any meaningful surge in demand hits an extremely thin liquid market.

**The miner revenue question.** As new supply shrinks, the long-term sustainability of Bitcoin mining shifts increasingly toward transaction fees. Currently, miners earn both freshly-minted coins and fees from users. Post-halving, the block reward is **3.125 BTC**, down from 6.25 BTC before April 2024. Critics argue fee revenue alone may not sustain network security at scale; optimists counter that rising adoption and higher BTC prices will more than compensate. This debate will only intensify through the 2028 halving.

**Why this matters for your clients.** The 20 millionth Bitcoin milestone is arriving while spot Bitcoin ETFs are absorbing hundreds of millions of dollars weekly. It's arriving as sovereign wealth funds begin treating Bitcoin like a reserve asset. And it's arriving at a moment when fiat currency credibility is under pressure globally. The next 5% of supply — the final 1,000,000 BTC — will take more than 100 years to produce. The supply-side story for Bitcoin has never been more compelling.
