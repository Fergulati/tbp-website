### Week of November 9, 2026

---

The defining story this week is the fallout from Tuesday's midterm elections, where Democrats swept both chambers of Congress and delivered an immediate blow to crypto markets. Bitcoin fell to a low not seen in two months, trading near \$73,708 on November 7, while Ethereum collapsed below \$2,100, its lowest level against Bitcoin since July. The Democratic victory effectively ends the CLARITY Act in its current form, the market structure bill the industry spent over \$170 million backing, and positions Senator Elizabeth Warren to chair the Senate Banking Committee. Against that turbulence, Coinbase received full approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency for its national trust company, a historic milestone for federally regulated crypto custody in the United States; FinCEN and the Federal Reserve withdrew a proposed rule that would have expanded remittance reporting requirements to cover crypto transfers at or above \$250; and Binance confirmed it will close accounts in four EU countries by December 31 as the MiCA compliance window closes. Our deep dive this week covers the real world asset tokenization market, which reached approximately \$40 billion in October 2026, and examines the structural challenges that will determine whether that figure can scale toward the trillions analysts project by 2030.

## 1. Bitcoin and Ethereum Drop to Two Month Lows as Markets Process the Midterm Results

Bitcoin fell to approximately \$73,708 on November 7, its lowest level since early September, before stabilizing near \$75,866 by November 8. Ethereum dropped harder in relative terms, falling below \$2,100 with \$343 million in long liquidations recorded in a single session. The ETH to BTC ratio reached its lowest point since July, marking a week of sustained pressure on the second largest asset.

The selloff followed the Democratic Party's sweep of both chambers in Tuesday's elections, an outcome markets read as a setback for the near term regulatory outlook. Traders who had positioned for a more favorable legislative environment under a Republican Congress began unwinding those positions immediately after results were called. Funding rates shifted negative on major perpetual exchanges, indicating continued caution rather than a sharp reversal heading into the week.

Bitcoin remains above its 200 day exponential moving average and has not broken the broader structural uptrend that defined most of 2026. Ethereum's relative underperformance reflects ongoing concerns about its positioning as institutional competition for real world asset custody grows across multiple blockchain networks. Analysts note that absent a macro catalyst or a clear legislative signal, the market is likely to consolidate in the current range before finding its next direction.

## 2. Democrats Win Both Chambers: The Crypto Regulatory Outlook Shifts Dramatically

The Democratic sweep of both chambers of Congress fundamentally reshapes crypto's legislative path heading into 2027. The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, which passed the House 294 to 134 in July 2025 and failed a Senate procedural vote in September 2026, is now effectively dead in its current form. Industry analysts confirmed the outcome within hours of results being called, with CertiK's Stephan Muehlbauer stating the bill had "collapsed in its current form" and Forte's Nick Golub concurring.

Senator Elizabeth Warren is expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee, signaling expanded SEC oversight and heightened scrutiny of tokenized product offerings including Robinhood's tokenized stock initiative and other platforms that combine brokerage and custody functions. A legislative vehicle based on the Waters proposal, which vests broader authority in the SEC, is now the most likely path forward, though specifics and timing remain uncertain as the new Congress prepares to take office.

The industry spent \$170.5 million this cycle, with at least \$125.5 million concentrated on Republican candidates who lost. Fairshake and affiliated political action committees focused heavily on the Ohio and Texas Senate races. The result marks the largest lobbying loss in the industry's history from a single election cycle and forces a strategic reset as groups shift from pursuing deregulatory market structure legislation toward negotiating oversight terms with the incoming Democratic majority.

The GENIUS Act governing stablecoins carries bipartisan support and survives the transition but its implementation details now face new scrutiny under the incoming committee leadership. CFTC decisions on certain perpetual products also face immediate legal challenge under the new political environment.

## 3. Coinbase Receives Full OCC Approval for Its National Trust Company

Coinbase won full approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency for its national trust company charter, completing a process that began seven months earlier with conditional approval. The result makes Coinbase National Trust Company a federally regulated entity authorized to provide institutional custody services, operating under OCC oversight in the same charter class as established bank trust departments across the United States.

The approval is a first of its kind for a publicly traded crypto exchange and extends federally regulated custody access to Coinbase's institutional clients without the need for a separate license in each state. The timing carries significance for the broader market at a moment when custody frameworks are under increased scrutiny from regulators, particularly in the context of the GENIUS Act's stablecoin provisions and the OCC's expanding crypto banking access program.

Coinbase's national trust company operates under key restrictions including prohibitions on taking deposits and limits on direct lending. The charter authorizes custody of both digital and traditional assets, targeting the institutional channel that has become the fastest growing segment of Coinbase's overall revenue base. The approval arrives as the company navigates a Morgan Stanley downgrade from earlier this month and prepares for a more demanding regulatory environment under the incoming Congress.

## 4. FinCEN and the Federal Reserve Withdraw the Crypto Travel Rule Expansion Proposal

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network and the Federal Reserve jointly withdrew a proposed rule that would have expanded the Bank Secrecy Act's travel rule definition to include cryptocurrency transactions at or above \$250, lowering the reporting threshold from the existing \$3,000 level. The withdrawal ends years of industry pushback and closes a rulemaking process that has been open since 2020.

The existing \$3,000 threshold for recordkeeping and reporting requirements remains in place. The withdrawal removes a proposed compliance layer that industry groups argued would have imposed substantial operational costs on even routine consumer crypto transfers and remittances. Organizations including Coin Center and the Blockchain Association had consistently opposed the lower threshold as disproportionate to the compliance benefit.

The move arrives in the same week that the Democratic midterm sweep changes Washington's regulatory calculus. While market structure and stablecoin discussions will now proceed under a more skeptical Congress, the FinCEN withdrawal signals that the outgoing regulatory posture under the current administration still leans toward narrowing the compliance burden on crypto services before the transition. It also preserves the more workable \$3,000 standard that the industry has operated under for years.

## 5. Binance Confirms Account Closures in Four EU Countries Ahead of MiCA Deadline

Binance confirmed it will close accounts and suspend services in Belgium, France, the Netherlands, and Poland by December 31, 2026, following its failure to secure Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation compliant operating licenses across those markets ahead of the MiCA transition deadline. Account fees begin November 29 as a final notice mechanism for affected users, and the exchange has posted official guidance on its European blog instructing users to withdraw funds before the cutoff.

Binance holds a MiCA compliant license in France through BN Europe but that entity does not cover the full range of services the exchange offers, leaving significant product lines unlicensed in those jurisdictions. The affected user population spans hundreds of thousands of accounts across the four countries, representing one of the largest forced exit events in European crypto history.

The closures illustrate the central dynamic of MiCA's first full compliance cycle: the regulation has forced a bifurcation between exchanges that completed the licensing process and those that did not. Platforms including Coinbase, Kraken, and Bitstamp are fully licensed across the EU while Binance operates only in a subset of markets. The December 31 hard deadline was confirmed in Binance's own regulatory notices posted to its European user base, with no extension announced or expected.

## 6. UK FCA Opens DeFi Compliance and Cryptoasset Exclusion Consultations

The Financial Conduct Authority opened parallel consultations on decentralized finance compliance and cryptoasset exclusion frameworks, covering the period ahead of the planned October 2027 full regulatory regime. The consultations build on the FCA's Feedback Statement FS26/1 published in September 2026, which applied a "same risk, same regulatory outcome" principle to tokenized financial instruments across the market.

The DeFi consultation addresses how compliance obligations attach to protocols that operate without a central operator, a question that has been unresolved since the FCA's initial cryptoasset guidance. The cryptoasset exclusion consultation clarifies which assets fall outside the new regime entirely, with particular relevance for utility tokens and certain nonfungible token structures that do not function as financial instruments.

Together the two consultations set the terms for the October 2027 implementation deadline, by which point all firms providing regulated cryptoasset services in the UK must hold full FCA authorization. The DLT Digital Securities Sandbox remains open in the interim, providing a pathway to permanent authorization for firms willing to operate under sandbox conditions and demonstrating the FCA's intent to build on the sandbox experience rather than replace it. Custody consultation is planned for the first half of 2027, addressing the safeguarding frameworks that institutional participants have identified as a gap in the current structure.

## 7. Solana's Real World Asset Ecosystem Surpasses \$5 Billion

The Solana blockchain's real world asset ecosystem surpassed \$5 billion in total value in October 2026, driven by a coordinated wave of institutional product launches across the month. JPMorgan Asset Management deployed two tokenized money market fund products on Solana on October 22. Securitize launched tokenized US equities with Jump Trading as market maker on October 8. Ondo Finance introduced Intelligent Portfolios using BlackRock developed strategies on October 29. Physical gold tokenization through DGLD, trade finance instruments from PrimeRevenue, and the Fiserv and Bank of North Dakota Roughrider Coin for interbank settlements rounded out the month's activity.

The milestone was enabled in part by the activation of the Alpenglow consensus upgrade on October 21, developed by Anza, the Solana core team spinout. Alpenglow reduced transaction finality to a targeted 150 milliseconds from the prior 12.8 second average under the TowerBFT mechanism, positioning Solana as a credible settlement layer for latency-sensitive institutional applications.

Solana now ranks as the third largest network by on-chain real world asset value, behind Ethereum at approximately \$21.8 billion and BNB Chain at approximately \$6.1 billion. The broader real world asset market reached approximately \$40 billion across all blockchains in October, with US Treasuries at \$14.5 billion remaining the dominant asset class and tokenized equities growing over 2,000 percent in twelve months from a small base. This week's deep dive examines that full market in depth.

## DEEP DIVE: Real World Assets Reach \$40 Billion But the Hard Part Has Just Begun

*A Research Report on the Market That Could Reshape Finance, and What It Still Needs to Get There*

### What Tokenizing a Real World Asset Actually Means

Think of a traditional financial asset like a corporate bond sitting inside a bank's custodian vault. It earns yield, it has an owner of record, and it can be sold, but selling it requires phone calls, wire transfers, settlement delays of two to three business days, and intermediaries collecting fees at every step. Tokenization is like converting the ownership certificate for that bond into a digital token on a blockchain. The underlying bond stays in the vault, but the token can transfer between wallets anywhere in the world in seconds, can be split into fractions too small for traditional markets to handle efficiently, and can plug into automated lending protocols that accept it as collateral without any human at the desk. The vault is still there; the friction around the vault is what changes.

That is the promise that has driven the real world asset tokenization market from approximately \$2 billion in 2022 to approximately \$40 billion in October 2026, a 920 percent expansion over four years. It is also why the promise has not fully arrived yet: the hard problems are not technical. They are legal, structural, and commercial.

### A Market That More Than Doubled in 2026

The on-chain tokenized real world asset market started 2026 at approximately \$21.5 billion and approached \$40 billion around October 13, 2026, nearly doubling within ten months. The figures vary materially across trackers. RWA.xyz, which uses a narrow methodology and excludes stablecoins, shows approximately \$39.2 billion as of October 15. Token Terminal's broader approach reached \$46.3 billion on October 25. Bernstein Research, which includes private credit at origination, cited \$51 billion as of May 2026. The spread reflects genuine disagreements on methodology rather than any single tracker being wrong.

Growth has decelerated from the explosive rates seen through 2025. RWA.xyz recorded a roughly 15 percent gain over July through September 2026, implying approximately 60 percent annualized, well below the 240 to 260 percent annualized gains of the prior period. This is consistent with a market transitioning from early adoption hype toward a slower and more structural buildout. Chainalysis pegged the year-over-year growth rate at 135 percent through the second quarter of 2026.

Tokenized US Treasuries and money market funds remain the largest single category at \$14.54 billion across 76 products and approximately 58,700 holders. The segment crossed \$10 billion for the first time on February 11, 2026 and has grown roughly 125 percent year-over-year. BlackRock BUIDL sits at approximately \$2.2 to \$3.1 billion, Franklin Templeton BENJI at approximately \$2.5 to \$2.6 billion, and Circle USYC at approximately \$2.9 billion. Despite their scale, Treasuries' share of the total on-chain market shrank from 73.7 percent at the start of 2025 to 67.2 percent by the first quarter of 2026 as competing asset classes accelerated.

Tokenized equities are the fastest growing segment in percentage terms, expanding over 2,000 percent in roughly twelve months. Circle leads the tokenized stock category with a 35.2 percent share and Ondo Finance commands 47.4 percent of tokenized ETF capital. RWA perpetuals grew 40 times in just six months through mid-2026 and Hyperliquid's exchange generated \$65 billion in trading volume in April 2026 alone. Tokenized commodities, dominated 89 percent by XAUT and PAXG representing gold, reached approximately \$5.5 to \$8.1 billion with tokenized gold spot trading hitting \$90.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, exceeding all of 2025 combined. Private credit is the most contested segment: Bernstein's broad methodology shows approximately \$24 billion, led by Figure Technology Solutions at \$18 billion in platform assets, while RWA.xyz's narrower view shows only approximately \$5 billion active on-chain.

Key players by value include Ondo Finance at \$4.07 billion, Sky (the entity formerly known as MakerDAO) at approximately \$4.9 billion, Securitize at approximately \$3.9 to \$4.2 billion (which listed on the NYSE in July 2026 under the ticker SECZ), Franklin Templeton and Circle in the mid-two-billion range, and JPMorgan Kinexys processing over \$7 billion in daily settlement volume.

### The Multi-Chain Infrastructure That Makes It Work

Ethereum holds approximately \$21.8 billion, or 47 percent, of the on-chain real world asset market, more than three times the next largest network. The chain distribution by value in October 2026 reads: Ethereum at 47 percent, BNB Chain at 12 percent, Solana at approximately 10 percent, Stellar at approximately 9 percent, with XRP Ledger, Avalanche, and Polygon holding significant secondary positions across a total of 35 or more active networks.

The compliance token standards underpinning this infrastructure have consolidated around two primary choices. ERC-3643, also called the T-REX standard, dominates for institutional issuances on Ethereum, with 78 institutional members in its association including DTCC and Chainlink Labs. It provides modular runtime-upgradeable compliance and an on-chain identity registry through the ONCHAINID system. For the Solana ecosystem, Token-2022, with its Transfer Hook programs and freeze authority, is used by Securitize, Superstate, Ondo, and DGLD. A new minimalist standard, ERC-7943, entered the EIP review process in 2026 as a vendor-agnostic alternative designed for compatibility across Ethereum Layer 2 networks.

Cross-chain distribution relies on LayerZero for BlackRock BUIDL across seven or more chains and for Ondo's cross-chain bridge; Chainlink CCIP for Maple Finance; and Centrifuge's multi-adapter quorum using Wormhole, LayerZero, Axelar, and Chainlink with configurable thresholds. Despite this infrastructure buildout, friction remains: pricing gaps of 1 to 3 percent exist for identical assets across chains, and capital movement costs run 2 to 5 percent. Only approximately 14 to 15 percent of tracked on-chain real world asset value is deployed in DeFi protocols. More than 50 percent of reported value sits idle with no secondary transfer activity.

The legal-technical bridge between off-chain assets and on-chain tokens remains the ecosystem's most consequential unsolved problem. Most tokenized assets provide what practitioners call certificatory claims, meaning a contractual right against a custodian, rather than direct title transfer. Courts consistently prioritize centralized registries over distributed ledgers. No major deployment has yet solved on-chain governance for asset events like dividends or stock splits, and the risk of tokens whose underlying asset has been sold or transferred without on-chain reflection remains a recognized operational hazard.

### The Regulatory Moment: US in Retreat, EU Cautious, UK Iterating

The November 2026 midterm elections delivered the most consequential regulatory event of the year for real world asset tokenization in the United States. The collapse of the CLARITY Act removes the clearest legal framework for determining whether tokenized assets are securities or commodities, leaving that classification undefined through at least 2027. Without a legislative answer, institutional capital deployment into US-domiciled tokenized products faces legal uncertainty at precisely the moment when European and UK frameworks are offering defined, if demanding, compliance paths.

The European Union's MiCA regulation, fully applicable across member states in 2026, actually excludes the highest-value real world asset categories: bonds, fund units, and notes are financial instruments governed instead by MiFID II, the Prospectus Regulation, and the Central Securities Depositories Regulation. The EU's DLT Pilot Regime provides blockchain infrastructure exemptions but caps individual bond issues at 1 billion euros and total infrastructure at 6 billion euros, constraints that significantly limit large-scale institutional tokenization. A European Commission proposal to raise those caps to 100 billion euros was not adopted as of October 2026 with member states resisting full ESMA supervisory authority.

ESMA's September 2026 response to the European Commission's MiCA review recommends adopting an optional EU-level 28th regime, a purpose-built layer for issuing, transferring, and settling tokenized securities, warning that without it, national regulatory divergence could create separate national or technological silos across the bloc. The UK's FCA, as covered in this week's newsletter, applies a same risk and same regulatory outcome principle with a custody consultation planned for the first half of 2027.

The combined effect is a regulatory geography that is still forming. The EU and UK are iterating toward workable standards for securities tokenization, even if slowly. The US regulatory vacuum will push institutional activity toward those jurisdictions, at least in the near term.

### What the \$40 Billion Headline Understates and Overstates

The November 3, 2026 Drofa Comms and RWA Summit report, drawing on the RWA Summit Singapore from October 9, 2026, frames the maturity test for 2027 as four functional questions rather than a size benchmark. Can tokens actually be traded after issuance? Can they serve as collateral, reducing the pre-funding institutions must carry? Are ownership and redemption rights enforceable across borders and in bankruptcy? And do platforms generate enough repeat revenue to sustain themselves commercially?

These four questions correspond precisely to the structural gaps documented throughout the market. More than 50 percent of reported real world asset value sits idle with no secondary transfer activity. Only 14 to 15 percent of tracked on-chain value is deployed in DeFi protocols. Institutions cannot yet routinely use tokenized assets as collateral, eliminating one of the core efficiency arguments for tokenization in practice. And the revenue models for tokenization platforms remain thin, with most relying on issuance fees rather than on the trading and lending fees that would indicate genuine market activity.

Headline market forecasts span roughly two orders of magnitude: McKinsey's base case reaches approximately \$2 trillion by 2030, Morgan Stanley projects \$3 to \$10 trillion, BCG and ADDX estimate \$16.1 trillion, and Standard Chartered reaches \$30.1 trillion by 2034. The more conservative Keyrock and Securitize joint report from April 2026 projects a \$400 billion base case by 2030, implying roughly 65 percent compound annual growth from the current \$40 billion base. No credible source provides a specific 2027 interim figure, and the wide variance in long-range projections reflects fundamental disagreement about whether "tokenized real world asset" should count only on-chain distributed tokens or also off-chain registered assets.

The honest summary is this: the \$40 billion headline is real and represents genuine institutional participation from BlackRock, JPMorgan, Franklin Templeton, Ondo, and dozens of others. But much of that value is static Treasury holdings and private credit origination where tokenization amounts to recordkeeping efficiency rather than transformative liquidity. The segment that gets the market to the trillion dollar forecasts is not more Treasuries but resolved legal frameworks for secondary trading, enforceable cross-border ownership rights, and vault interfaces like ERC-7540 that allow permissioned tokens to plug into DeFi. The infrastructure foundation is there. The legal and commercial problems are what remain.
